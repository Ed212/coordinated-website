Launch example / Northstar

Illustrative workflow

A launch plan that keeps moving.

For GTM leads, product leaders and founders at 50 to 300-person software companies shipping faster than their go-to-market. Here is how an AI go-to-market program manager could help a team work through one scope change.

Coordinated is prelaunch. Northstar is fictional, and these cards show the workflow we’re building, including intended Slack interactions. They are not live software or customer results.

← Back to the homepage

The change

Security review delays Northstar’s export feature.

What is affected?

The launch plan includes export in package claims, the product demo, sales materials and support guidance. Engineering has not yet supplied the evidence Security needs to clear it.

What can move?

Marketing and Support can draft changes against approved scope. Product and Pricing must approve that scope and its claims before teams finalize materials. The core release must still pass its own gates.

What must wait?

Export stays blocked until Security clears it. Each team confirms the work it can deliver and by when. The release owner makes the go/no-go decision after the required gates pass.

01 · Establish the current plan

Which plan are people working from?

Start with the agreed launch date, approved product scope, package claims, owners and required gates. Keep the source of each decision attached to the plan.

Northstar: export clearance is pending

The current plan includes export. Security has not cleared it. Engineering owns the missing readiness evidence.

Next question for Product: should the core launch proceed without export?

The open gate stays visible. A forecast does not silently change the agreed launch plan.

02 · Find affected work

Who needs to work through the change?

Connect the export delay to Product scope, Engineering readiness, Pricing and packaging, Marketing content, the Sales demo and Support guidance.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Export appears in four launch materials

Pricing: package claims. Marketing: launch page. Sales: demo. Support: guidance.

These owners need to confirm the changes and delivery dates after Product decides the scope.

Finding a dependency starts a conversation. It does not create a new commitment for its owner.

Illustrative · Northstar

03 · Separate parallel work from gates

See what can move and what must wait.

Prepare options and drafts in parallel. Hold final claims for the relevant approvals, and hold export for Security clearance.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Draft now. Finalize after approval.

Marketing and Support can draft for a core-only launch. Product must approve scope; Pricing must approve package claims.

Export remains blocked. Core release gates still apply.

A shorter path through the work does not remove a required review or approval.

Illustrative · Northstar

04 · Put the decision with a person

Product chooses the scope. Owners confirm delivery.

Product approves a core-only launch. Pricing approves revised claims. Marketing, Sales and Support then confirm what they can change and by when.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Core-only scope approved

Marketing and Support have confirmed revised materials for Thursday. Sales’ demo confirmation is still open.

Next owner: Sales. Confirm the revised demo and delivery date.

Approval of scope and confirmation of delivery are separate answers. Silence counts as neither.

Illustrative · Northstar

05 · Carry approved changes

Keep the agreed plan current as answers arrive.

The plan records the approved scope and claims, the owners’ confirmed dates, and the evidence still needed. Conflicting or missing answers stay open.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Sales confirms the revised demo

The core-only demo will be ready Thursday. Marketing, Sales and Support have confirmed their affected work.

Engineering still needs to supply export evidence. Security clearance remains pending.

The plan distinguishes a promised update from evidence that the update was delivered.

Illustrative · Northstar

06 · Make the release decision

What is ready, what is blocked, and who decides next?

In this example, the core release gates pass and revised materials are checked. The release owner approves the core-only launch. Export waits for its separate Security clearance.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Core launch: approved to proceed

Ready: approved core scope, package claims, demo, launch content and support guidance. Required core gates: passed.

Blocked: export. Next owners: Engineering supplies evidence; Security reviews it. The release owner decides its later release.

Illustrative ending: a human go/no-go decision, confirmed owners and an explicit remaining blocker.

After the launch

Check what happened next.

Did owners deliver the agreed work? Did the launch achieve its intended result? Check those separately, with named owners and evidence. Keep unanswered questions visible and agree the next review.
