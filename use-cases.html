Illustrative workflow

Launch is a milestone. Keep learning.

For GTM leads, product leaders and founders at 50 to 300-person software companies. Follow the planned Slack workflow from a growth bet through dated commitments, owner check-ins, launch gates and the next agreed move.

Coordinated is prelaunch. Northstar and its people are fictional. These cards show the workflow we’re building, including intended Slack interactions, rather than live software or customer results.

The growth bet

Help new teams reach their first shared report.

What are we trying?

Northstar plans a guided activation flow for team workspaces, with clearer messaging and a customer walkthrough. The team hopes more new teams will reach a useful first report.

What must come together?

Product scope, positioning, pricing, budget and launch gates set the agreed boundaries. The first-run flow, customer invitations and support guidance need owners, dates and explicit dependencies.

What would we learn?

Do new teams understand the promise and reach a useful report? Agree which observations would inform that question, who will gather them and when people will review them.

Illustrative · Northstar

01 · State the bet and the assumptions

What growth outcome is this work meant to support?

People choose the audience, product promise and growth ambition. The proposed program manager helps make the assumptions, evidence questions and review date explicit.

Northstar: guided activation

A growth ambition is a hypothesis to test. It is not evidence of growth or a promised lift.

02 · Form dated owner commitments

Who will deliver what, and by when?

Turn the goal into concrete work with a named owner, a date and a clear completion check. Ask each owner to confirm the proposed commitment and its dependencies.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Three proposed commitments need answers

Maya, Product: first-run flow and readiness evidence by Fri Nov 13. Leo, GTM: launch message and demo by Thu Nov 12.

Priya, Support: a guide by Fri Nov 13, after approved scope and messaging. Leo’s exact acceptance and the launch-gate owner are still open.

A proposal is not an accepted commitment. Silence does not confirm an owner, a date or permission to act.

03 · Run targeted check-ins

Ask about the gap that affects the next step.

Check with the responsible person when a missing answer, dependency or readiness check affects the plan. Keep the question specific and carry the response into the agreed work.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Leo: can you accept this exact work and date?

The message and demo are proposed for Thu Nov 12, against the approved activation scope. Priya’s guide depends on the approved message.

Can you accept that work and date? Who owns the launch-readiness gate? Both unanswered questions stay visible.

If export’s security review slips, people can assess a narrower scope while draft work continues. Export still waits for clearance, and changed claims wait for approval.

04 · Launch through the gates

Readiness evidence informs a human release decision.

Product approves scope; GTM approves positioning. Pricing approves commercial terms; Finance approves budget. Security gates, owner acceptance and the release owner’s go/no-go remain separate.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Version 1: the approved launch plan

The activation scope, positioning, pricing and budget are approved. Maya, Leo and Priya have accepted their exact work and dates. Dana owns the launch-readiness gate.

Dana checks that the required gates passed and approves the core launch for Tue Nov 17. Export remains outside the approved scope, held for Security clearance.

Confirmation that work is delivered, approval to release and acceptance of a specific action are separate steps.

05 · Gather evidence against the bet

The launch happened. Did the expected behavior follow?

Named people bring selected sales, customer, marketing and product observations to the agreed review in Slack. Each supplies the source date and observation window. The proposed workflow compares those signals with the original assumptions, keeping missing coverage and competing explanations visible.

CoordinatedIllustrative Slack card

Tue Nov 24: the evidence is incomplete

Leo’s notes, updated Mon Nov 23, cover Tue Nov 17 to Mon Nov 23. They suggest the next-step prompt may be confusing. Some customer responses and Product observations are missing; the guided walkthrough itself may explain the positive responses.

These notes do not establish growth. Question for the team: test a clearer prompt, change positioning, or gather more evidence first?

Earlier interview notes stay dated background, not current proof of this test. A separately agreed test could begin with named people and selected Slack sources, with source permissions and evidence ownership agreed first.

Illustrative · Northstar

06 · Decide and follow through

Carry the next human decision into confirmed work.

On Tue Nov 24, Product and GTM approve testing a clearer next-step prompt. Their decision weighs incomplete evidence alongside product judgment; it does not approve budget or accept delivery work.

Illustrative ending: a human decision and targeted questions for follow-through. The growth hypothesis remains open; approval and delivery acceptance remain separate.

An ongoing loop

Plan. Coordinate. Learn. Decide what comes next.

A launch is one milestone in product-to-market work. Check delivery separately from market response, keep the original assumptions visible and bring the next choice to the people with authority to make it.

We’re building shared versions of agreements for people and permitted agents to consult, with sources, evidence gaps and unanswered questions. People could see accepted work; agents could read approved claims and constraints. Reading grants neither approval nor action authority.
