Our principles

Everyone deserves to understand
the work they are part of.

Coordinated is built around a simple belief: the people doing the work deserve context, a voice in the commitment, and an explanation when the direction changes.

01

The reason travels with the work.

People should be able to see why their contribution matters, how it connects to the company’s direction, and which assumptions support it.

What this asks of the productConnect the commitment to its goal, reasoning, and source. Revisit the assumptions when new evidence arrives.

02

People help shape the commitment.

The owner needs a chance to explain what is possible, propose conditions, or raise a concern while a plan is taking shape.

What this asks of the productKeep assignment, acknowledgment, and delivery acceptance distinct. Silence never becomes consent.

03

Input gets an answer.

A concern should remain connected to the decision, including how it was addressed. Being heard does not mean every proposal is adopted.

What this asks of the productShow whether input was adopted, addressed another way, declined with a reason, or remains unresolved. Keep the person informed.

04

Honest updates keep the plan useful.

People should be able to report a changed forecast without pretending an agreement has already changed.

What this asks of the productPreserve the agreed terms alongside new information. Make open questions visible in the review; never rank people by response speed.

05

Visibility has an audience.

People should know who can see their contribution, concern, and supporting context. Participation should not require exposing unrelated work.

What this asks of the productStart with selected initiatives and explicit sources. Make the audience clear before an answer is shared.

06

People retain authority.

AI can help gather context, find missing information, and prepare options. People decide which commitments to make and which trade-offs to accept.

What this asks of the productName the decision-maker. Preserve their rationale and any unresolved disagreement. An agent reading a plan is not proof that its actions followed it.

A shared plan takes
an ongoing conversation.

These principles guide the experience we are building and testing with design partners. A pilot is where we check whether they make the work clearer and the coordination lighter.
