Without Coordinated

Your launch lead holds the threads together.

  1. Discover the impact. Read the change, find affected teams and ask who owns each dependency.
  2. Work out the order. Separate core preparation that can continue from claims and release work waiting on decisions.
  3. Coordinate the plan. Obtain approvals, check owners and evidence, resolve conflicting commitments and keep everyone working from the agreed scope.

With Coordinated, as we’re building it

A program manager helps work it through.

  1. Build the impact map. Use approved scope, feature, pricing and security information to propose affected owners and dependencies.
  2. Move the coordination forward. Ask responsible owners, check readiness evidence and prepare options. Keep missing answers and blockers visible.
  3. Maintain the agreed plan. Coordinate revised commitments as people approve changes. Teams still do their work; humans still decide.

01 / The first launch

Manage the work of the launch.

We’re building a program manager that works from the current launch plan, checks what is needed and coordinates the work through to readiness.

  1. Build the current plan

    Bring existing documents, goals and accountable owners into one current launch plan.

  2. Map what must happen

    Find missing owners, approvals, launch gates and dependencies. Separate parallel work from work that must happen in sequence.

  3. Check readiness

    Check with responsible owners and review readiness evidence against each gate and commitment.

  4. Bring decisions forward

    Surface blockers, options and judgment calls. People make consequential decisions and approvals; owners accept their work.

  5. Keep the plan moving

    Coordinate affected commitments when scope, positioning or dates change, then check delivery and results.

02 / Approved launch context

One current plan. Approved information behind it.

Approved positioning, feature and security information would underpin the launch plan, with current versions and accountable owners. It can guide people and agents as they coordinate the work. Over time, we intend to extend that company context to other consequential decisions.

People keep authority.

Decision approval, owner acceptance and action permission stay separate. Required specialist gates still apply.

Guidance is scoped.

People and agents see permitted context. Reading guidance neither authorizes nor enforces action.

Private stays private.

Share permitted context or message Coordinated directly. This does not open other people's private DMs.

Decision roles and authority

DACI names a human Driver, scoped Approver, Contributors and Informed people. Participation grants no rights. The human Driver and planned Driver agent are different actors. Specialist gates, each owner's feasible acceptance and permission to act remain separate.
