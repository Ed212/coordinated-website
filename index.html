A growth bet needs a plan to act and learn.

Help new teams reach their first shared report. Northstar wants to test whether guided activation makes that first step clearer. The hypothesis needs dated commitments, launch gates and a review of what people actually experience.

Form the commitments

A goal becomes work people accept.

  1. Name the outcome and assumptions. Product and GTM agree the audience, the growth bet and what evidence would challenge it.
  2. Propose the work and dates. Maya owns the first-run flow by Fri Nov 13. Leo owns the message and demo by Thu Nov 12. Priya owns support guidance by Fri Nov 13.
  3. Get exact acceptance. Each owner confirms the deliverable and date. Missing gate owners and dependencies stay open until the responsible people answer.

The planned Coordinated workflow

Keep the program moving after launch.

  1. Check the answers that matter. Targeted owner check-ins cover open commitments, readiness evidence and dependencies. Silence is not acceptance.
  2. Launch through the required gates. Product approves scope. Pricing approves commercial terms and claims. Specialists keep their gates; the release owner makes the go/no-go call.
  3. Review market evidence. On Tue Nov 24, named owners bring dated interview notes and updates to compare with the original assumptions. Missing coverage and competing explanations stay visible. People decide what to test next.

Launch is a milestone, not the end.

The intended loop connects the agreed plan, owner check-ins, release and market learning. Coordinated would prepare the next options and affected work. People bring taste, context and judgment; owners then accept the resulting work and dates.

How it works

From an agreed bet to the next agreed move.

This is the program we’re designing: form the commitments, run targeted check-ins and carry learning into coordinated follow-through.

  1. Start with a goal and named people

    Agree the audience, assumptions and responsible people. An initial pilot could use named owners and selected Slack sources, only where separately permitted.

  2. Form a feasible plan

    Make the deliverable, owner, date, dependencies and launch gates concrete. Ask owners to accept the exact work rather than treating a proposed date as agreement.

  3. Check where an answer is needed

    Ask the relevant owner about open work or evidence. Surface missing answers, blockers and judgment calls in the current plan.

  4. Examine the market response

    Named owners supply dated sales, customer, marketing and product observations at agreed review windows. Compare them with the assumptions, including gaps and competing explanations.

  5. Decide, then coordinate

    People choose what changes next. Confirm affected owners, exact acceptance and dates, then follow through and review again.

The agreement behind the work

One agreed direction for people and agents.

Conversation history helps you recall what was said. We’re building Coordinated to show what was agreed and decided, which version applies, and what still needs an answer.

That shared direction would connect positioning, pricing, budgets, dependencies and launch gates to the active plan. When a decision changes, Coordinated would find the affected commitments, bring the questions to the right people and follow through on accepted changes.

For the people doing the work

Know the accepted work, current commitments and unresolved decisions. See what changed and which owners still need to accept it.

For agents you permit

A permitted agent preparing a launch draft would consult the applicable approved claims, version and sources, and flag missing answers.

Reading is not approval

Read access grants no action or approval authority. People keep decisions and required approvals; owners still accept the exact work and dates.
