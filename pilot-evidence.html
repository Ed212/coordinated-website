How we’ll measure

Proposed checks

Check the work. Test the growth bet.

A useful GTM program manager should help people agree concrete work, coordinate it through launch, then learn from market response. We would test that loop rather than assume that shipping more produces growth.

Coordinated is prelaunch, with no launched users or customer results. Any future pilot needs separately agreed scope, permissions and readiness. We would agree the starting point, owners and review dates before evaluating it.

Six questions

01

Did the goal become concrete commitments?

Check whether each piece of work has a named owner, a date, a clear completion check and known dependencies. Distinguish proposed work from what the owner confirmed. Record missing owners or dates rather than treating them as agreed.

02

Did targeted check-ins resolve useful questions?

Check whether owner questions surfaced the missing answer, dependency or evidence while people could act. Count unanswered questions, unnecessary interruptions and corrections too. A seen message is not a confirmed commitment.

03

Did delivery and launch gates hold?

Compare delivered work with the agreed scope, claims and dates. Check readiness evidence and required approvals separately. Record the authorized release decision and any acceptance needed for a specific action; do not infer one from another.

04

How much human effort did the whole loop take?

Count setup, owner answers, decisions, evidence gathering, reviews, corrections and follow-through, including work our team performs by hand. Compare equivalent scope and constraints. Do not treat a shorter meeting or fewer messages alone as proof of value.

05

What did market evidence say about the assumptions?

Agree the intended behavior, named evidence owners and review window before the test. Ask for dated sales, customer, marketing and product observations where permitted. Keep source dates, observation windows, missing coverage and competing explanations visible. Separate delivery from the intended business outcome.

06

Did the next decision become confirmed follow-through?

Check what people chose after reviewing the evidence, who had authority and which owners accepted the next work and dates. Product taste, strategy and constraints still matter. Report whether the plan changed, stayed the same or needs more evidence.

The approach

Agree the test before the work.

Write down the growth hypothesis, scope, starting point, evidence questions and review dates with the relevant owners. A first test could begin with named people and selected Slack sources only where separately permitted. Agree readiness, evidence sources and data handling first. Label manual work and prototype behavior clearly.

Growth is the outcome ambition, not a proven lift. A before-and-after change alone does not establish cause. Report limitations and inconclusive evidence alongside observations; do not present illustrative examples as measured results.
