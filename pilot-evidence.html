How we’ll measure

Proposed checks

Check the work. Test the growth bet.

We would test whether agreed work gets delivered and market evidence informs the next decision. Shipping alone does not establish growth.

Coordinated is prelaunch, with no launched users or customer results. Any future pilot needs agreed scope, permissions and readiness before evaluation.

Six questions

01

Did the goal become concrete commitments?

Check whether each piece of work has a named owner, a date, a clear completion check and known dependencies. Distinguish proposals from the exact work and date each owner accepted. Record missing answers rather than treating them as agreement.

02

Did targeted check-ins resolve useful questions?

Check whether owner questions surfaced the missing answer, dependency or evidence while people could act. Count unanswered questions, unnecessary interruptions and corrections too. A seen message is not a confirmed commitment.

03

Did delivery and launch gates hold?

Compare delivered work with the applicable approved scope, positioning, pricing, budget and dates. Check each decision with the person authorized to make it. Keep required Security gates, readiness evidence, the release decision and exact action acceptance separate.

04

How much human effort did the whole loop take?

Count setup, owner answers, decisions, evidence gathering, reviews, corrections and follow-through, including work our team performs by hand. Compare equivalent scope and constraints. Do not treat a shorter meeting or fewer messages alone as proof of value.

05

What did market evidence say about the assumptions?

Agree the intended behavior, named evidence owners and review window before the test. Ask for dated sales, customer, marketing and product observations where permitted. Keep source dates, observation windows, missing coverage and competing explanations visible. Check that old notes are not presented as current proof. Separate delivery from the intended business outcome.

06

Did the next decision become confirmed follow-through?

Check which changes people approved and which owners accepted exact work and dates. Distinguish applicable agreements from proposals, superseded decisions and unaccepted work. In a separately permitted test, could people and agents identify the version that applies, its sources and unanswered questions? Reading must grant no approval or action authority.

The approach

Agree the test before the work.

Agree the growth hypothesis, starting point, evidence questions and review dates with the relevant owners. A first test could use named people and selected Slack sources where separately permitted. Agree data handling and label manual work and prototype behavior clearly.

Growth is the outcome ambition, not a proven lift. A before-and-after change alone does not establish cause. Report limitations and inconclusive evidence alongside observations; do not present illustrative examples as measured results.
