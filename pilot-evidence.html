Pilot evidence / Definitions before results

Measure the hours after a plan changes.

A pilot starts with one live initiative and defines the measures before the first change. The readout shows where coordination load moved, how quickly a recorded decision arrived, and whether the next review opened current.

← Back to the homepage

Five pilot measures

01

Chase load per week

The number of direct follow-ups the operator sends to obtain an owner, answer, condition or decision for the pilot initiative. Routine reminders unrelated to a recorded change are excluded.

[Chases per week: before → after]

02

Hours from reported change to recorded decision

Elapsed time from the first artifact or tagged thread that reports a change until the person with authority records the accepted terms. Forecasts that never become changes remain visible but do not count as decisions.

[Median hours to decide]

03

Changes decided before the earliest affected deadline

The number of recorded changes decided before the first downstream lock, review, customer date or spend deadline named in the accepted dependencies.

[Changes decided before the deadline: n of m]

04

People pending at the deadline

The count of people asked to accept, condition, propose a date or decline who have not answered when the earliest affected deadline arrives. People who were only informed are not included.

[People pending at deadline]

05

Review preparation minutes

Operator time spent reconstructing the current initiative state before the weekly review. Time spent deciding future work during the review is excluded.

[Review prep: before → after]

Method

The definition does not move after the result arrives.

The operator confirms the initiative, the earliest affected deadlines and the baseline window before the pilot begins. Coordinated records timestamps from accepted cards and review briefs. The final readout keeps bracketed fields until there is enough observed data to replace them.

No invented benchmark, extrapolated saving or unnamed customer quote appears in the readout.
