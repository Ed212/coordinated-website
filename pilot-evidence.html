Working definitions to finalize at kickoff
Measure What we record Proposed success test
Recurring operator effort Minutes spent chasing, reconstructing state, preparing the brief, and correcting product output. Record founder assistance separately. At least 30% lower than a comparable baseline. Report setup time alongside recurring savings.
Critical changes All changes with an affected deadline, their first shared report, authority, disposition, and timestamps. Every critical change decided or explicitly escalated before the deadline. Escalation is not counted as resolution.
Participant burden Total response and correction minutes, requests sent, unnecessary interruptions, and a short participant check. No increase in participant effort; any missing or disputed representation is corrected and kept visible.
Integrity of the record Pending responses, disagreements, missing evidence, and the link between input and the authorized decision. No unanswered request presented as consent; no forecast presented as an approved amendment.
Agent experiment, if scoped Which version was available, fetched, attached to an artifact, and checked before an action. Report those stages separately. Claim enforcement only for the covered action path with a verified gate.

The 30% threshold is a starting proposal, not a promise. Keep counts and elapsed time alongside percentages. Small samples support a decision about this workflow, not a universal performance claim.

At the third review

Continue. Adjust. Or stop.

Decide together. A good pilot should make that choice easier.

01 / Continue

The value survives the accounting.

Recurring coordination is lighter, participant burden has not risen, the record is trustworthy, and the operator wants to use it for the next cycle.

02 / Adjust

There is one specific thing to fix.

A concrete workflow or accuracy issue prevents value. Agree the change and a short retest; don’t extend indefinitely on enthusiasm.

03 / Stop

The burden outweighs the benefit.

Too much correction, displaced work, insufficient need, or no evidence of improvement. Record why and close the pilot on the agreed terms.

What the readout must disclose

Count what happened.
Name what didn’t.

Include baseline dates, actual workload, setup and founder support, participant effort, open decisions, and missing observations. Show decisions and escalations separately.

A rehearsal is labeled a rehearsal. A fetched plan is not an enforced action. Finishing a release is not proof that its business hypothesis held.

Customer names, quotes, and results appear only with permission. Until then, the website examples remain clearly illustrative.
