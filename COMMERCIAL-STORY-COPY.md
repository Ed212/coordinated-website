### Less waiting. Less rework. A next check.

A general assistant can help draft a plan or recall a conversation. Coordinated is being designed around a shared, versioned plan: what people decided, which work each owner accepted and what still needs an answer.

1. **Build the plan with the people**

   Start with the goal and team. Bring positioning, pricing, content, budget, owners, dependencies and launch gates into one plan. Ask the responsible people to resolve open choices.

2. **Work from the current agreed version**

   Keep people and permitted agents working from current decisions and accepted commitments. When scope changes, show what it affects and ask those owners to accept the revised work.

3. **Give learning a next step**

   After launch, bring customer observations, market evidence and actual spend to a review. In the fictional Northstar example, public agency forum questions shared by the team suggest an opportunity. Maya checks whether the product could support a sellable offer; Leo checks buyer need, strategic fit and Northstar’s presence in search and AI answers. Fit remains uncertain, so Dana and GTM choose interviews before changing positioning, scope or spend. Leo accepts three buyer interviews by Fri Dec 4; Maya accepts a capability summary for the Tue Dec 8 review. They compare dated findings from Tue Dec 1 through Fri Dec 4 before deciding whether to develop an offer, adjust positioning or pass. The opportunity remains an unproven hypothesis.

### People stay accountable.

Shared decisions and accepted commitments are the intended foundation. People keep judgment, approval and delivery responsibility.

#### People approve and revise decisions

People can revise positioning, pricing, budgets and company standards using judgment and necessary approvals. Required specialist gates remain in place.

#### Owners accept the work

Named owners accept the exact deliverable and date. A decision approval is not delivery acceptance; missing answers remain open.

#### Agents stay within permissions

Agents would use only permitted sources and actions. Reading context grants no approval or action rights. Publishing needs separate authorization.
