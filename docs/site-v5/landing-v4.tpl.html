The question we ask at formation

Which recurring meeting does this launch retire, and from when?

The hours above come from that answer, and from nothing else. If every meeting stays, Coordinated adds work. The sponsor answers it on day one, the default being that the Monday picture replaces the status meeting from week three once it has been right for two, and the readout says whether the meeting stayed retired.

What it never does

The refusals are the product.

Every other tool produces more for you to read. Coordinated produces acceptances, prepared decisions, and a quiet week that says what it checked. Each line below is enforced by code, not by a model's judgment.

  • Never predicts a person, never scores people. No risk labels, no health colours, no ranking, no sentiment.
  • Never invents a path, a party or a date. Options come from your answers and your record; every date on a card is a date on record.
  • Never contacts a customer. What a customer was told is recorded in the words of the person who heard it, and re-checked with that person.
  • Never writes to another system. Read-only, by rule and by code: no write tool can be registered.
  • Only a person's acceptance makes a term real. A decision creates cards to accept, not acceptance. Until the owners answer, the previous terms stand and the note says so.
  • Silence is never consent. Nothing is accepted by a timeout. A decision past its deadline goes to the person you named for that, by the rule you set at formation.
  • Reads nothing you did not hand it, post in a channel it has claimed, or tag it in. No channel summaries, no reading of direct messages or meetings.
  • No nudges, no "just checking in", no reminder of any kind. A second ask about the same thing is refused unless the record changed.
  • Counts in channels, never your terms. Each person sees only their own card. No app to check: it lives in Slack.
  • An attention budget per person, agreed at formation, with escalation rules. Nothing waits in a digest if a launch depends on it.

For the architecture reader

Three agents, one record, guardrails as code.

Everything above rests on one object: the record of what your company agreed, that people and agents read the same, and that only people can change. This is how it is kept true.

The driver, the operator's agent

The program manager. It prepares before it asks: it reads what you already have, proposes owners, dates, dependencies and the decisions nobody has made, each with the source behind it, then interviews each owner alone and reads their terms back as a card they accept. It keeps the Monday picture, opens a case when a move crosses the rule set at formation, asks only the people whose accepted terms are exposed, and builds options only from their answers. Its objective is the least human effort per dependable commitment, inside a mandate the operator grants and can pause.

The verifier, the record's agent

The keeper of evidence. Before any message reaches a person it attaches, to every claim, the acceptance that made it true, the evidence that supports it with the period it covers, and the right that authorises it. A claim it cannot support is qualified or removed if no decision needs it, and refused with an objection if one does. It verifies a condition only when a decision needs it, tells accepted from recently verified from unknown on every Monday picture, and routes a second-hand report to its owner instead of recording it. Its core is a lookup in the record, not a model's opinion, and it runs on a different model from the driver.

The aligner, the sponsor's agent

It watches the distance between the accepted terms and the outcome the sponsor stated, and the decisions past their derived deadline. When an accepted change touches the outcome statement or the bet behind it, the sponsor gets a drift event as information, never a block. It prepares the outcome decision from the evidence on record, and its second job is to say when that evidence suggests the objective itself needs reconsidering. Three agents with one objective would agree with each other and be wrong together; these three want different things, and a disagreement they cannot settle in three rounds goes to a person.

Guardrails as code

No guardrail is enforced by a model. Thirty-six deterministic checks, G1 to G36, are pure functions over the message or the change and the record: same input, same verdict, no network, no model, each with a passing and a failing test. They run on every change to the record, on every message before it can be sent, and on every scheduled tick. A violation never reaches a person; it becomes a refusal event with the rule id, and the readout counts refusals by rule. Among them: only a person's acceptance makes a term real (G1); nothing invented (G5); silence is never consent (G6); provenance on every claim (G14); the attention budget (G16); nothing goes outside the company (G20); no writes to other systems (G21); models never write (G32).

Provenance on every message, and version control for agreements

The run record is an append-only, hash-chained log of events; replaying it reproduces the record to the byte, and the readout is computed from events by the engine, never by a model summarising its own work. Every term carries its revisions. Acceptance is per person, per term, per revision; a card issued against an old revision cannot act on a new one; a decision creates cards to accept, not acceptances; the next phase inherits the history and none of the acceptances. That is version control for what your company has agreed, a different object from version control for tasks or for documents: who accepted this line, at which revision, on what basis, under which conditions, and who may change it.

Who can say yes, as data

A decision right is granted by a person to a person, with a scope, bounds and an expiry, and it counts only once the holder accepts it. A decision outside the bounds is refused by code; a conflict in authority is shown, never resolved by the product. Governance, in this design, is a lookup rather than a meeting: every agent, human or software, can ask what is current, who may change it, what counts as done, and how much attention it may spend.

Your agents can read it: who decides, what was agreed, what changed since.

A read-only server over the record, speaking the Model Context Protocol, with provenance on every answer: the acceptance or evidence that makes each answer true, and the record's hash at that moment. The tools, in the order agents need them:

  • who_decides
  • open_decisions
  • commitments
  • impact_preview
  • constraints
  • what_changed_since
  • plan_as_of
  • current(kind)

The one write is propose, which routes to the person who holds the right and can never accept. An agent about to draft a campaign reads current('positioning') first; an agent about to move a milestone asks who_decides before it does. Canonical documents enter the record as pointers with a single decider, never as content; the document stays where it lives, and the record says which version the company is executing against and who may change that.
