Appendix / Design record

The site is a record before it is a campaign.

The visual system uses ledger structure, a single changed fact in red, and real HTML product cards. Claims stay bracketed until a pilot supplies evidence.

← Back to the homepage

Sitemap and rationale

Homepage establishes the category, demonstrates one change, explains the record, and ends with the pilot and trust boundary. Use cases gives operators six complete moments with cards and provenance. Pilot evidence defines measurement before results exist. Design notes keeps the design choices, prompts and source ledger inspectable.

The separation keeps the homepage crisp while giving a diligence-minded buyer enough detail to verify how the product behaves and how a pilot will be judged.

Three hero concepts

01 · Editorial and calm — built

┌ announcement proof ─────────────────────┐
│ headline + promise │ real Slack card    │
│ two actions        │ on ledger drawing │
└ trust boundary ─────────────────────────┘

Chosen because it makes the product object legible in the first viewport and matches the quiet authority of the voice. The generated field supports the card without pretending to be the product.

02 · Kinetic instrument

red margin records scroll
v1 ───── v2 ───── v3
headline shifts only when terms change

The red margin would become a live version timeline. Rejected for launch because the mechanism would compete with the card before visitors understand what is being recorded.

03 · Monday-to-Tuesday narrative

Mon Oct 5, 9:14 AM · forecast
↓ cards reach Sarah and Marco
Tue Oct 6, 9:05 AM · decision receipt

The full hero would advance from forecast to decision as the visitor scrolls. Rejected because it delays the one-line category explanation and depends on motion for comprehension.

Headline alternatives

  1. Every affected owner answers before the date moves.
  2. The current plan starts with what people accepted.
  3. Changes reach the people whose work depends on them.
  4. What changed, who answered, what became real.
  5. The next review opens with the current terms.

Each stays under twelve words and avoids software-development vocabulary.

Signature interaction

Reader receipt — built

As major sections enter view, the receipt near the bottom records their labels and prints a weekday-stamped local time. The state stays in the browser and is never sent; it turns the site's own principle into an interaction without inventing product capability.

Ideas not built

  • Draggable date: useful, but recomputing affected owners from fictional dependencies risks making a deterministic demo look like a product claim.
  • Scroll-scrubbed red margin: expressive, but it would make the change color decorative.
  • Hover-only moment cards: compact on desktop, but it hides essential content from keyboard and touch users.

Visual system and assets

Style sentence: precise ink-line drawings on warm ledger paper with one red element for the changed fact, no gradients and no glow.

Every generated asset, alt text and exact prompt is recorded in ASSET-PROMPTS.md. Product cards are HTML, never images. The existing two-track mark is reused unchanged.

Fact ledger

  • “Half of committed sprint work ships when promised” — LinearB, 2,000+ engineering teams.
  • “45% of product launches slip by a month or more” — Gartner product-manager survey.
  • “87% say there is no capacity to coordinate once everyone is executing” — Atlassian State of Teams 2026, 12,000 workers.
  • All Northgate, Atlas, Acme, people, dates and cards are fictional examples supplied or constructed for product demonstration; they are not customer evidence.
  • All pilot outcomes, quotes, names and measures remain bracketed placeholders.
